About dates and rules

CAP dates, fees and some rules are set again every year by the State CET Cell. This guide uses the 2026-27 cycle as its worked reference and says so wherever a detail is year-specific. Before you act, check this year's notices on cethub.in/updates and the official documents on cethub.in/resources.

1. The big picture

CAP reaches you as a stream of separate notices, but it is one pipeline. Knowing its shape makes every notice easier to place.

#StageWhat happens
1CAP registrationA separate registration for admission, after the result. Taking the exam does not enrol you.
2Document upload and verificationYour category, domicile and qualification documents are checked (e-scrutiny or physical scrutiny).
3Merit listsA provisional merit list, a short grievance window, then the final merit list used for every round.
4Option formYour ordered list of college + branch choices. In the 2026 cycle each round had its own option-form window.
5AllotmentThe system gives you the highest choice on that round's list that your merit position reaches.
6Accept and reportEvery allotted candidate completes self-verification, seat acceptance and reporting within that round's window.
7Further roundsRounds repeat until the final round; then vacant seats move to institute-level admission.
Where most trouble starts

Students focus on the option form and neglect documents. An incomplete or rejected document set can leave you in the wrong category on the merit list, and that follows you into every round.

2. CAP registration

After the MHT-CET result, you register for admission on that year's official CAP portal (in 2026 this was fe2026.mahacet.org; the current portal is linked from cethub.in/resources). This creates your CAP profile, separate from your exam profile. In 2026, registration opened on 2 July, about two weeks after the PCM result (15 June).

Common misconception

"I wrote the exam, so I'm already in CAP." This is false. If you do not register for CAP in its window, you cannot take part in the CAP rounds.

Keep your name, date of birth and marks exactly as they appear on your Class X and XII documents. Mismatches are a common cause of verification delays. CAP registration can carry its own application fee, which differs by category and is set in each year's notice.

3. Document upload and verification

This is where your claimed category, domicile and qualification are checked against real documents. Two modes are usually offered:

ModeHow it worksSuits
e-ScrutinyUpload documents on the portal; they are verified online.Most candidates with clear, consistent documents.
Physical scrutinyDocuments checked in person at a Facilitation Centre.Candidates with discrepancies or claims that need explaining.

Typical documents (check this year's brochure)

  • MHT-CET scorecard for the current year
  • Class X and Class XII mark sheets and passing certificates
  • School or college leaving certificate
  • Domicile certificate, or the proof the brochure accepts instead
  • Aadhaar (Aadhaar authentication was mandatory for CET registration in 2026)
  • Category documents where applicable: Caste Certificate, Caste/Tribe Validity Certificate, Non-Creamy-Layer certificate, EWS certificate
  • Gap certificate, if there is a gap after Class XII

The companion guide "Category & Reservation Documents Checklist" covers category documents in detail.

Worked example

Priya claims OBC but uploads only her Caste Certificate, without the Non-Creamy-Layer certificate. Her claim cannot be verified, so she risks appearing under OPEN on the provisional merit list until she fixes it in the grievance window. Category claims need the complete set, not one certificate.

4. Provisional and final merit lists

After verification, a provisional merit list ranks all eligible candidates, followed by a short grievance window to correct errors: a wrong category, a missing document that you did submit, or an incorrect score. In 2026 the provisional list came out on 22 July with grievances open until 25 July, and the final merit list followed on 28 July.

The merit list is a single statewide ranking. Your category position is worked out from it, so an error in your category or details shifts where you stand for every seat type.

Always check your provisional entry

Errors do happen. The grievance window is the easy time to fix them; once the final list is out, corrections are much harder.

5. The option form, every round

The option form is your ordered list of college + branch choices. The allotment can only give you a seat that is on your list, so this is the most consequential thing you do in CAP.

In the 2026 cycle, each round had its own option form

You did not fill one list for the whole process. Before each round, the vacant seats were published and a fresh option-form window opened: Round II on 7 to 9 August, Round III on 20 to 22 August and Round IV on 30 August to 1 September. If you want to be considered for a better seat in a round, submit and confirm that round's form inside its window. Check this year's notice for how the rounds work.

How to build a strong list (zones, ordering, a worked example and how auto-freeze should change your top choices) is covered in the companion guide "CAP Option Form Strategy".

6. Allotment, seat acceptance and reporting

After each round's option form closes, the provisional allotment is published. If you are allotted a seat, you act within that round's window. In 2026 every allotted candidate had to:

  1. Complete self-verification on the portal.
  2. Accept the seat and pay the seat acceptance fee. In 2026 this was ₹1,000, paid once at your first acceptance (Admission Notice 5). A July notice that would have raised it for later acceptances was cancelled.
  3. Report to the allotted institute within the reporting window, with original documents, and pay the fees the institute asks for.
Missing a window usually means losing the seat

The 2026 windows were short: Round I acceptance and reporting ran from 3 to 5 August, Round II from 13 to 18 August, Round III from 25 to 28 August and Round IV from 4 to 7 September. Treat them as seriously as the exam.

TFWS candidates

Tuition is waived, but other charges such as development or ancillary fees may still be payable. Confirm the break-up with the institute and carry your income certificate when you report.

7. Freeze, Float and auto-freeze

When you accept a seat, you also choose whether to stay in the running for something better:

OptionWhat it does
FreezeYou keep this seat and leave the later rounds.
Float (Not Freeze / betterment)You keep this seat as a safety net and stay eligible for a higher choice in later rounds. If a better seat is allotted, it replaces this one; if not, you keep this one.
Slide (where offered)Like Float, but only for a better branch at the same institute. Check whether this year's notice offers it.
Auto-freeze: some seats are frozen for you

In the 2026 cycle, a seat was frozen automatically if it matched one of your top preferences for that round: your 1st preference in Round I, one of your first 3 in Round II, or one of your first 6 in Round III. An auto-frozen candidate pays the acceptance fee, reports, and takes no part in later rounds. So only put choices you would be happy to end with at the top of each round's list.

8. The final round and after CAP

  • Round IV is final. In 2026, whatever seat you held after the Round IV allotment was your final CAP seat.
  • Institute-level admission: seats still vacant after CAP are filled by institutes through a separate process with its own rules and timeline. In 2026, institutes had to follow the brochure's rules and publish their merit lists.
  • Final admission date: every admission has a hard closing date. In 2026 it was revised to 14 September after a Supreme Court order and the revised AICTE calendar.

10. Common pitfalls

  1. Assuming the exam registers you for CAP. CAP registration is separate and has its own window.
  2. Incomplete category documents. A Caste Certificate alone does not establish an OBC, SEBC, VJ or NT claim; the Non-Creamy-Layer certificate is also needed.
  3. Not checking the provisional merit list. Errors are easy to fix in the grievance window and hard afterwards.
  4. Skipping a later round's option form. In 2026 each round had its own window; missing it meant not being considered for a better seat that round.
  5. Putting a "maybe" college at the top. With auto-freeze, a top choice you are only lukewarm about can become your final seat.
  6. Missing acceptance or reporting. Both can cost you the seat, however good your rank.
  7. Assuming institute-level rounds work like CAP. They have their own rules and timelines.

11. Quick checklist

  • I registered for CAP after my result, not just for the exam.
  • My complete document set, including category documents, is ready before verification opens.
  • I checked my provisional merit list entry and raised any errors in time.
  • I know this year's option-form window for every round and have reminders set.
  • I understand Freeze, Float and auto-freeze, and my top choices each round are seats I would be happy to keep.
  • I know the acceptance fee, the documents to carry and each round's reporting deadline.
  • I am following cethub.in/updates for this year's notices.
